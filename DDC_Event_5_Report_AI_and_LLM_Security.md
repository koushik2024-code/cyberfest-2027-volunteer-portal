## 7. Summary of the Event (Session-wise Technical Breakdown)

### 7.1. Session Overview & Agenda (10:00 AM – 10:10 AM)
* The session opened with introductory remarks on behalf of DDC, SASIGD, and IEEE SSIT, highlighting how generative AI systems have created unprecedented attack vectors that bypass traditional software perimeters.
* Speaker Sandeepraj Gatla was introduced to the audience, citing his background in enterprise SOC incident handling.

### 7.2. Core Technical Proceedings (10:10 AM – 11:05 AM)
1. **The Expanding Risk Surface of AI Systems (10:10 – 10:35 AM):**
   - Structural differences between deterministic code and probabilistic generative AI models.
   - Deconstructing **Prompt Injection Attacks**: Direct prompt injection, indirect prompt injection via retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) feeds, and goal hijacking.
   - **System Prompt Extraction & Data Leakage:** Methods attackers use to exfiltrate proprietary instructions, sensitive embeddings, and unintended PII.
   - **Model Poisoning & Adversarial Perturbations:** Subverting model alignment, manipulating fine-tuning weights, and safety guardrail evasion.
2. **Real-World Incident Response & Case Studies (10:35 – 10:55 AM):**
   - High-volume incident triage: how attackers exploit AI agent plugins and tools.
   - Applying the **MITRE ATLAS (Adversarial Threat Landscape for AI Systems)** matrix alongside enterprise MITRE ATT&CK.
3. **Defensive Architectures & Monitoring Tooling (10:55 – 11:05 AM):**
   - Logging, anomaly detection, and prompt filtering using SIEM/EDR platforms (CrowdStrike Falcon, Splunk).
   - Implementing input guardrails, semantic firewalls, and least-privilege agent design.

### 7.3. Interactive Q&A and Career Guidance (11:05 AM – 11:15 AM)
* High student engagement regarding career opportunities in AI Red Teaming, the significance of emerging certifications (CLLMSP, CEH), and transition from traditional IT into DFIR.
* Distribution of participation certificates and closing remarks.
